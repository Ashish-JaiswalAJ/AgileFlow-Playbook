# Product Backlog & Product Backlog Refinement

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how AgileFlow should understand, explain, refine, and work with:

- Product Goal
- Product Backlog
- Product Backlog Items (PBIs)
- Product Backlog ordering
- PBI description and sizing
- Product Backlog refinement
- decomposition and splitting
- dependencies
- readiness for Sprint selection
- the distinction between PBIs and user stories

The central rule is:

> Do not impose user stories, acceptance criteria, story points, Definition of Ready, specific tools, templates, or refinement meetings as mandatory Scrum requirements.

Classification: [SCRUM] unless otherwise specified.

---

# 2. Product Goal

The Product Goal describes a future state of the product that can serve as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against.

The Product Goal is contained in the Product Backlog.

The Scrum Team should fulfill one Product Goal before taking on the next.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Agent Guidance

When helping a user plan or organize a Product Backlog, AgileFlow should consider the relationship:

Product Goal
→ Product Backlog
→ Product Backlog Items
→ Sprint selection
→ Increment

Do not invent a Product Goal when the user's product objective has not been established. If necessary, ask for clarification.

---

# 3. Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product.

It is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.

The Product Backlog evolves as the product and its environment are understood better.

Classification: [SCRUM]

## Key characteristics

The Product Backlog is:

- emergent
- ordered
- transparent
- continuously evolving
- composed of Product Backlog Items

The backlog is not necessarily a fixed requirements document created completely at the beginning of a project.

---

# 4. Product Backlog Items

Product Backlog Items (PBIs) represent work that may contribute to improving the product.

A PBI can contain information such as:

- description
- order
- size

The specific representation of a PBI is not prescribed by Scrum.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Important distinction

A Product Backlog Item is a Scrum concept.

A user story is one possible way of representing a requirement or PBI.

Therefore:

> PBI = [SCRUM]

while:

> User Story = [PRACTICE]

Do not use these terms as if they were automatically identical.

---

# 5. User Stories and PBIs

User stories are a commonly used Agile technique for representing requirements.

A PBI may be represented as a user story, but Scrum does not require the user-story format.
