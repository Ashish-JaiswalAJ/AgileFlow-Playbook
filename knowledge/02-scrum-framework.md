# Scrum Framework

## 1. Scrum Definition

Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems.

Scrum is intentionally incomplete. It defines the essential elements of the framework while allowing teams to use other techniques and practices.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

## 2. Empiricism

Scrum is founded on empiricism.

Empirical process control is based on knowledge coming from experience and making decisions based on what is observed.

Scrum relies on three pillars:

1. Transparency
2. Inspection
3. Adaptation

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Transparency

Important aspects of the process must be visible and understandable to those performing the work and those receiving the results.

Examples include:

- Product Backlog
- Sprint Goal
- Sprint Backlog
- Definition of Done
- Increment

Poor transparency makes meaningful inspection difficult.

### Inspection

Scrum artifacts and progress toward goals should be inspected frequently enough to detect undesirable variations or problems.

Inspection should not become so frequent that it interferes with the work.

### Adaptation

When inspection reveals that an aspect of the process or resulting product is outside acceptable limits, the process or materials should be adjusted as soon as possible.

Adaptation makes Scrum useful in complex and changing environments.

## 3. Scrum Values

A successful use of Scrum depends on five values:

- Commitment
- Focus
- Openness
- Respect
- Courage

### Commitment

The Scrum Team commits to achieving its goals and supporting one another.

### Focus

Everyone focuses on the work of the Sprint and the goals of the Scrum Team.

### Openness

The Scrum Team and stakeholders are open about the work and the challenges involved.

### Respect

Team members respect one another as capable and independent people.

### Courage

Team members have the courage to do the right thing and work on difficult problems.

Classification: [SCRUM]

# 4. Scrum Team

The Scrum Team consists of:

- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Developers

There are no sub-teams or hierarchies within the Scrum Team.

The Scrum Team is:

- cross-functional
- self-managing
- focused on a common Product Goal

The team is responsible for creating a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 5. Product Owner

The Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team.

The Product Owner is accountable for effective Product Backlog management, including:

- developing and communicating the Product Goal
- creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog Items
- ordering Product Backlog Items
- ensuring the Product Backlog is transparent, visible, and understood

The Product Owner may delegate Product Backlog management work, but remains accountable for the result.

The Product Owner is one person, not a committee.

### Important boundary

Scrum does not define the Product Owner as:

- a manager who assigns developer tasks
- the person who must personally perform every Product Backlog activity
- the sole person who provides all product knowledge

Developers and stakeholders also contribute information and collaboration.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 6. Scrum Master

The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide and for helping the Scrum Team become effective.

The Scrum Master helps:

- establish Scrum practices
- improve team effectiveness
- coach team members in self-management
- remove or facilitate the removal of impediments
- ensure Scrum events occur and remain purposeful
- help the organization understand Scrum

### Important boundary

The Scrum Master is not automatically:

- the team's project manager
- the team's boss
- the person who assigns every task
- personally responsible for solving every impediment

The Scrum Master helps create conditions in which the Scrum Team can manage its own work effectively.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 7. Developers

Developers are the people in the Scrum Team who are committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint.

Developers are accountable for:

- creating a plan for the Sprint, the Sprint Backlog
- instilling quality by adhering to the Definition of Done
- adapting their plan each day toward the Sprint Goal
- holding one another accountable as professionals

Developers decide how selected work gets done.

### Important boundary

Scrum does not require the Product Owner or Scrum Master to assign individual development tasks.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 8. Scrum Events

Scrum defines five events:

1. Sprint
2. Sprint Planning
3. Daily Scrum
4. Sprint Review
5. Sprint Retrospective

The Sprint is the container for all other Scrum events and activities.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 9. Sprint

A Sprint is a fixed-length event of one month or less during which a usable and valuable Increment is created.

A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous Sprint concludes.

During the Sprint:

- the Sprint Goal is protected
- quality does not decrease
- the Product Backlog is refined as needed
- scope may be clarified and renegotiated as more is learned
- the Sprint Backlog can be adapted

### Sprint length

Scrum does not require two-week Sprints.

A team may use a shorter or longer Sprint length, provided it remains within Scrum's maximum one-month duration.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 10. Sprint Goal

The Sprint Goal is the single objective for the Sprint.

It provides flexibility regarding the exact work needed to achieve the objective.

The Sprint Goal is created during Sprint Planning and is added to the Sprint Backlog.

### Important boundary

A Sprint should not become a collection of unrelated tasks without a coherent objective.

The selected work should support the Sprint Goal.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 11. Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed.

Sprint Planning addresses three topics:

### Why is this Sprint valuable?

The Product Owner proposes how the product could increase its value and utility in the current Sprint.

The whole Scrum Team collaborates to define a Sprint Goal.

### What can be Done?

Developers select Product Backlog Items to include in the Sprint.

Selection is based on the Sprint Goal and the Developers' understanding of their capacity and ability to complete the work.

### How will the chosen work get done?

Developers plan the work necessary to create an Increment that meets the Definition of Done.

The resulting plan forms the Sprint Backlog.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Important boundary

Sprint Planning does not require:

- individual task assignment by the Product Owner
- story points
- Planning Poker
- a particular Sprint Planning template
- a two-week Sprint

These may be team practices but are not mandatory Scrum requirements.

Classification: [PRACTICE] / [OPTIONAL] / [TEAM]

---

# 12. Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog consists of:

1. Sprint Goal
2. Selected Product Backlog Items
3. An actionable plan for delivering the Increment

The Sprint Backlog is a plan by and for the Developers.

It is a highly visible, real-time picture of the work that Developers plan to accomplish during the Sprint.

The Sprint Backlog is updated throughout the Sprint as more is learned.

### Important boundary

The Sprint Backlog is not necessarily frozen after Sprint Planning.

Changes can occur while still protecting the Sprint Goal.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 13. Daily Scrum

The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for the Developers of the Scrum Team.

Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary.

Developers determine the structure and techniques used for the Daily Scrum.

### Important boundary

Scrum does not require the traditional three questions:

1. What did I do yesterday?
2. What will I do today?
3. Are there any blockers?

Those questions are one possible technique.

The Daily Scrum should not become merely a status report to the Scrum Master or Product Owner.

Classification: [SCRUM] for the event and purpose  
Classification: [PRACTICE] for the three-question format

---

# 14. Increment

An Increment is a concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal.

An Increment must be usable.

Multiple Increments may be created within a Sprint.

Work that does not meet the Definition of Done is not considered part of the Increment.

If multiple Increments are created during a Sprint, they work together as a coherent product.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Core rule

Usable + meets Definition of Done = Increment

---

# 15. Definition of Done

The Definition of Done is a formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product.

When a Product Backlog Item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is created.

If work does not meet the Definition of Done, it is not considered Done.

The Definition of Done creates transparency regarding the quality of the Increment.

Classification: [SCRUM]

A team may define additional quality standards, provided they do not contradict the applicable Definition of Done.

---

# 16. Sprint Review

The Sprint Review is held at the end of the Sprint to inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations.

The Scrum Team presents the results of their work to key stakeholders and discusses:

- what was accomplished
- changes in the product environment
- what should be done next
- possible Product Backlog adaptations

The Product Backlog may be adjusted based on what is learned.

### Important boundary

The Sprint Review is broader than a demonstration.

A demonstration can be part of the Review, but the purpose is inspection and collaboration about the product and future direction.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 17. Sprint Retrospective

The Sprint Retrospective concludes the Sprint.

Its purpose is to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.

The Scrum Team inspects:

- individuals and interactions
- processes
- tools
- Definition of Done
- other relevant aspects of the way the team works

The team identifies the most useful changes and may add improvements to the Sprint Backlog for the next Sprint.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Important boundary

A Retrospective should not become:

- a blame session
- a meeting with no actionable improvement
- a generic discussion disconnected from evidence

---

# 18. Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product.

It is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team.

Product Backlog Items contain information such as:

- description
- order
- size

The Product Backlog evolves as the product and its environment are understood better.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 19. Product Goal

The Product Goal describes a future state of the product that can serve as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against.

The Product Goal is contained in the Product Backlog.

The Scrum Team should fulfill one Product Goal before taking on the next.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 20. Product Backlog Refinement

Product Backlog refinement is the activity of breaking down and further defining Product Backlog Items into smaller and more precise items.

Refinement can include:

- adding detail
- removing ambiguity
- breaking down items
- reordering items
- resizing items
- improving transparency

Refinement is an ongoing activity.

### Important boundary

Scrum does not define Product Backlog Refinement as a separate mandatory Scrum event.

A team may schedule refinement meetings if useful.

Classification:
- Refinement activity: [SCRUM]
- Specific refinement meeting/cadence: [PRACTICE] / [TEAM]

---

# 21. Scrum Does Not Prescribe Every Development Practice

Scrum defines the framework, but teams may use additional practices appropriate to their context.

The following are not automatically mandatory Scrum requirements:

- User stories
- Acceptance criteria
- Given/When/Then
- Definition of Ready
- Story points
- Planning Poker
- Fibonacci estimation
- T-shirt sizing
- Velocity
- Burndown charts
- Jira
- Trello
- Azure DevOps
- Two-week Sprints
- Three Daily Scrum questions
- TDD
- Continuous Integration
- Specific testing frameworks
- Specific programming languages
- Production deployment every Sprint

AgileFlow must classify these appropriately rather than calling them required Scrum elements.

Classification: [PRACTICE] / [OPTIONAL] / [TEAM]

# 22. Scrum Mental Model

When answering a Scrum question, use this sequence:

1. Identify the Scrum concept involved.
2. Determine whether it is formally defined by Scrum.
3. Identify the relevant Scrum accountability, event, artifact, or commitment.
4. Distinguish mandatory framework elements from complementary practices.
5. Consider the user's context.
6. Identify missing information.
7. Give practical guidance without inventing requirements.

# 24. Final Rules

AgileFlow must never invent Scrum requirements.

In particular, it must not automatically state that:

- user stories are mandatory
- acceptance criteria are mandatory Scrum artifacts
- story points are required
- Planning Poker is required
- Fibonacci is required
- Definition of Ready is required
- Sprints must be two weeks
- Daily Scrum must use three questions
- refinement must be a weekly meeting
- the Product Owner assigns developer tasks
- the Scrum Master is the team's manager
- the Sprint Backlog cannot change
- every Sprint must produce a production release
- velocity is an individual performance metric

When a practice is not formally required by Scrum, explain its classification and context instead.

### Core principle

> Scrum provides a framework for empiricism and adaptive product development. It does not prescribe one complete software-development process.
