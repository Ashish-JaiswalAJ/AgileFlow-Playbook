# Agile Foundation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the Agile foundation used by AgileFlow.

AgileFlow should distinguish Agile principles from the Scrum framework and from complementary Agile practices.

---

## 2. What Is Agile?

Agile is an approach to software development based on iterative delivery, collaboration, customer value, responding to change, and continuous learning.

Agile is not a single methodology, tool, ceremony, or project-management template.

AgileFlow should not equate Agile with Scrum. Scrum is one framework used within the broader Agile context.

---

## 3. Agile Manifesto Values

The Agile Manifesto identifies four value preferences:

1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
4. Responding to change over following a plan

The preference on the left does not mean the item on the right has no value. The emphasis is on the items on the left.

Classification: [AGILE]

---

## 4. Twelve Agile Principles

The Agile principles emphasize:

1. Satisfying the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
2. Welcoming changing requirements, even late in development.
3. Delivering working software frequently.
4. Maintaining close collaboration between business stakeholders and developers.
5. Building projects around motivated individuals and providing the environment and support they need.
6. Using effective communication, especially direct communication, to convey information.
7. Treating working software as a primary measure of progress.
8. Maintaining a sustainable pace of development.
9. Continuously paying attention to technical excellence and good design.
10. Maximizing simplicity by avoiding unnecessary work.
11. Supporting self-organizing teams because effective solutions and architectures can emerge from them.
12. Regularly reflecting on how to become more effective and adapting behavior accordingly.

Classification: [AGILE]

---

## 5. Agile Does Not Prescribe One Workflow

Agile does not by itself require:

- Scrum
- Kanban
- User stories
- Story points
- Planning Poker
- Fibonacci estimation
- Two-week iterations
- A specific project-management tool
- A specific backlog template
- A specific testing framework
- A specific programming language
- A specific ceremony schedule

These may be useful practices depending on context, but they should not automatically be described as Agile requirements.

Classification: [AGILE] / [PRACTICE] / [OPTIONAL] depending on the specific technique.

---

## 6. Agile and Scrum

### Agile

Agile provides values and principles that emphasize:

- customer value
- collaboration
- working outcomes
- frequent delivery
- responding to change
- sustainable development
- technical excellence
- reflection and adaptation

Classification: [AGILE]

### Scrum

Scrum is a lightweight framework for helping people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems.

Scrum provides a defined framework including:

- Scrum Team
- accountabilities
- events
- artifacts
- commitments
- empiricism
- Scrum values

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Important distinction

Agile and Scrum should not be treated as interchangeable terms.

Correct:

> Scrum is an Agile framework.

Avoid:

> Agile requires Scrum.

---

## 7. Agile Mindset

When helping users apply Agile, AgileFlow should emphasize:

### Value over activity

Completing more tasks does not automatically mean creating more value.

Consider whether the work contributes toward a valuable product outcome.

### Feedback over assumptions

Teams should use feedback and evidence to improve their understanding of the product and adapt their approach.

### Collaboration over isolated handoffs

Agile emphasizes collaboration among the people involved in developing and using the product.

### Adaptation over rigid plans

Plans are useful, but new information can require changes.

Changing a plan is not automatically an Agile failure.

### Working outcomes over activity reports

Progress should be connected to usable product outcomes rather than only completed activities.

### Continuous improvement

Teams should regularly inspect how they work and identify improvements.

---

## 8. Agile Planning

Agile does not mean "no planning."

Agile teams can plan:

- product direction
- upcoming work
- Sprint work
- technical activities
- testing
- releases
- experiments
- improvements

The important distinction is that plans can be adapted when new information becomes available.

AgileFlow should avoid presenting Agile as either:

> "Plan everything in advance"

or:

> "Do not plan."

Instead, explain planning as an adaptive activity.

Classification: [AGILE]

---

## 9. Agile Documentation

Agile does not mean "no documentation."

The Agile Manifesto values working software over comprehensive documentation, but documentation can still provide value.

Useful documentation depends on:

- product needs
- regulatory requirements
- technical complexity
- team communication
- maintenance needs
- stakeholder needs

AgileFlow should not recommend eliminating documentation simply because a team follows Agile.

Classification: [AGILE] / [PRACTICE]

---

## 10. Agile and Continuous Improvement

Agile emphasizes regular reflection and improvement.

A team can improve by:

- identifying problems
- examining evidence
- discussing possible causes
- experimenting with changes
- observing results
- adapting its process

AgileFlow should distinguish:

**Observation → Interpretation → Action**

Example:

Observation:
> Stories frequently remain unfinished at the end of the iteration.

Interpretation:
> Possible causes could include oversized work, unclear requirements, dependencies, or unexpected technical complexity.

Action:
> Inspect the evidence and choose an appropriate improvement experiment.

Do not automatically claim a single cause without evidence.

Classification: [AGILE]

---

## 11. Source Boundary

For formal Scrum definitions, AgileFlow should use the Scrum Guide as the primary authority.

For Agile values and principles, AgileFlow should use the Agile Manifesto and its principles.

Agile practices that are not formally defined by these sources should be classified separately rather than presented as mandatory requirements.

---

## 12. Core Rules for AgileFlow

AgileFlow must:

- distinguish Agile from Scrum
- distinguish values and principles from specific practices
- avoid treating common practices as universal requirements
- preserve the user's context
- avoid inventing requirements
- identify assumptions when necessary
- distinguish observations from interpretations
- support adaptation based on evidence
- explain trade-offs when relevant
- avoid presenting one Agile process as universally correct

### Core principle

> AgileFlow should help users apply Agile thinking appropriately rather than force every team into the same Agile recipe.
