# Inspection & Adaptation

## 1. Purpose

This document explains inspection and adaptation in Agile/Scrum.

It covers:

- empiricism
- transparency
- inspection
- adaptation
- evidence-based decisions
- Sprint-level inspection
- Product Backlog adaptation
- Sprint Backlog adaptation
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective
- responding to change
- troubleshooting
- continuous improvement
- common misconceptions

The central rule is:

> Agile and Scrum rely on transparency, inspection, and adaptation rather than assuming that the original plan will remain correct.

---

# 2. Empiricism

Scrum is founded on empiricism.

Empiricism means that knowledge comes from experience and observation, and decisions are made based on what is known.

Scrum implements empiricism through:

- transparency
- inspection
- adaptation

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 3. Transparency

Transparency means that important aspects of the process must be visible to those responsible for the outcome.

Examples include transparency of:

- Product Backlog
- Sprint Backlog
- Increment
- Definition of Done
- progress toward the Sprint Goal
- current product information

Classification: [SCRUM]

AgileFlow should encourage users to make relevant information visible rather than relying on hidden assumptions.

---

# 4. Inspection

Inspection means examining Scrum artifacts and progress toward a goal frequently enough to detect potentially undesirable variances or problems.

Inspection should not become excessive or disruptive.

Classification: [SCRUM]

AgileFlow should distinguish:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Inspection
    ↓
Identify variance / problem
    ↓
Adapt
````

---

# 5. Adaptation

When inspection identifies something that requires change, the process or work can be adapted.

Adaptation should happen as soon as possible when further deviation could create a larger problem.

Classification: [SCRUM]

Examples include:

* adapting the Sprint Backlog
* clarifying Product Backlog Items
* changing future Product Backlog ordering
* improving the Definition of Done
* addressing process problems
* changing the team's way of working

---

# 6. Transparency → Inspection → Adaptation

The three concepts work together:

```text
Transparency
     ↓
Information is visible
     ↓
Inspection
     ↓
Current situation is examined
     ↓
Adaptation
     ↓
Work/process is changed when necessary
```

AgileFlow should avoid treating these as isolated concepts.

---

# 7. Inspection Is Not Prediction

Inspection is based on what is currently known.

It does not require the team to predict the future perfectly.

For example:

```text
Plan created
      ↓
Work begins
      ↓
New information appears
      ↓
Current situation inspected
      ↓
Plan adapted
```

A change to the plan is not automatically evidence that the original plan was useless.

It may simply reflect improved knowledge.

---

# 8. Adaptation Does Not Mean Random Change

Adaptation is not uncontrolled change.

An adaptation should respond to information discovered through inspection.

AgileFlow should help distinguish:

```text
Evidence-based adaptation
```

from:

```text
Unstructured change
```

When possible, identify:

* what changed
* what evidence revealed the change
* what impact it has
* what adaptation is being considered
* what goal must remain protected

---

# 9. Sprint-Level Inspection

During a Sprint, the team continuously inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal.

The Daily Scrum provides a formal opportunity for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog.

Classification: [SCRUM]

Other inspection activities may also occur as part of the team's way of working.

Classification: [PRACTICE]

---

# 10. Daily Scrum and Adaptation

The Daily Scrum is not only a progress update.

Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary.

Developers can use the event to:

* inspect progress
* coordinate
* identify necessary changes
* adapt their plan

Classification: [SCRUM]

The exact conversation format is not prescribed.

---

# 11. Sprint Backlog Adaptation

The Sprint Backlog can evolve during the Sprint.

As Developers learn more, they can update the plan.

This may include:

* changing the implementation approach
* adding or removing tasks
* adjusting the plan
* clarifying work
* adapting selected scope where appropriate

Classification: [SCRUM] / [PRACTICE]

The Sprint Goal remains the central focus.

---

# 12. Sprint Goal as a Boundary

Adaptation during a Sprint should preserve the Sprint Goal.

For example:

```text
New information
      ↓
Inspect impact
      ↓
Adapt Sprint Backlog
      ↓
Protect Sprint Goal
```

If the Sprint Goal remains valuable and achievable, the plan can change while continuing the Sprint.

If the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete, Sprint cancellation may become relevant.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 13. Product Backlog Adaptation

The Product Backlog is emergent.

As the product and its environment are better understood, the Product Backlog can change.

Adaptation may involve:

* adding new PBIs
* removing obsolete PBIs
* clarifying PBIs
* splitting large PBIs
* changing ordering
* changing estimates or sizing where the team's method supports it

Classification: [SCRUM] / [PRACTICE]

The Product Owner is accountable for Product Backlog management.

---

# 14. Sprint Review as Inspection and Adaptation

The Sprint Review provides an opportunity to inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations.

The Scrum Team and stakeholders collaborate to inspect:

* what was accomplished
* what changed in the product environment
* what should be done next

The Product Backlog may be adapted based on what is learned.

Classification: [SCRUM]

### Important boundary

The Sprint Review is broader than a demonstration.

A demo may be included, but inspection and adaptation are central purposes.

---

# 15. Sprint Retrospective as Inspection and Adaptation

The Sprint Retrospective provides an opportunity for the Scrum Team to inspect how the Sprint went.

The team can inspect:

* individuals and interactions
* processes
* tools
* Definition of Done
* other relevant aspects of its way of working

The team identifies the most useful changes to improve effectiveness and quality.

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 16. Product Inspection vs Process Inspection

Different Scrum activities inspect different things.

### Product-focused inspection

The Sprint Review primarily examines the Sprint outcome and product context.

### Process-focused inspection

The Sprint Retrospective focuses on how the team worked and how effectiveness and quality can improve.

Classification: [SCRUM]

AgileFlow should not treat the Review and Retrospective as interchangeable.

---

# 17. Evidence-Based Adaptation

When recommending an adaptation, AgileFlow should identify the evidence behind it.

Use:

```text
Observed fact
     ↓
Interpretation
     ↓
Possible cause
     ↓
Adaptation
```

Do not silently convert an interpretation into a fact.

Example:

```text
Observation:
Three PBIs were not completed.

Possible interpretations:
- PBIs were too large
- requirements were unclear
- unexpected complexity occurred
- dependencies delayed work
```

These possibilities require further inspection.

---

# 18. Avoiding Root-Cause Assumptions

AgileFlow should not immediately state:

> "The team failed because requirements were bad."

That is a conclusion, not an observation.

Instead:

> "Three PBIs were not completed. Possible contributing factors include item size, unclear requirements, dependencies, or unexpected complexity. More evidence is needed to determine the cause."

Classification: [UNKNOWN] when the cause has not been established.

---

# 19. Inspection Questions

When helping a team inspect a problem, AgileFlow can ask:

### What happened?

Identify observable facts.

### What was the goal?

Identify the relevant objective.

### What changed?

Identify differences between expectations and current reality.

### What evidence do we have?

Separate facts from assumptions.

### What might explain the difference?

Generate possible causes.

### What can be adapted?

Identify possible changes.

### What should remain protected?

Identify the relevant goal or quality constraint.

---

# 20. Adaptation Questions

Before proposing an adaptation, AgileFlow should consider:

1. What information triggered the adaptation?
2. What goal is being pursued?
3. What changes if the adaptation is made?
4. What risks or trade-offs exist?
5. Is the proposed change within the team's authority?
6. Is the change temporary or structural?
7. How will the result be inspected?

AgileFlow should avoid presenting one adaptation as automatically correct when the evidence is incomplete.

---

# 21. Inspection During Refinement

Product Backlog refinement can be used to inspect and improve understanding of upcoming work.

The team may inspect:

* unclear requirements
* item size
* dependencies
* assumptions
* ordering
* missing information

The results can lead to adaptation of Product Backlog Items.

Classification: [PRACTICE]

Refinement is not a separate mandatory Scrum event.

---

# 22. Inspection of Product Backlog Items

When reviewing a PBI, AgileFlow should inspect:

* intended outcome
* clarity
* scope
* dependencies
* ambiguity
* size
* relevant acceptance conditions
* assumptions

If information is missing, AgileFlow should identify the gap rather than inventing an answer.

---

# 23. Inspection of Sprint Progress

When a user asks:

> "Are we on track?"

AgileFlow should avoid giving a confident conclusion based only on one metric.

Relevant information may include:

* Sprint Goal progress
* selected work
* remaining work
* current blockers
* quality state
* dependencies
* newly discovered information

Velocity or burndown may be used if the team already uses them, but they are optional practices.

Classification: [OPTIONAL] / [PRACTICE]

---

# 24. Metrics and Inspection

Metrics can provide information, but a metric should not automatically become the definition of success.

Examples of optional metrics include:

* velocity
* burndown
* cycle time
* defect counts

The Playbook emphasizes distinguishing formal Scrum elements from complementary practices.

AgileFlow should not introduce a metric merely because it is commonly used.

Classification: [OPTIONAL] / [PRACTICE]

---

# 25. Metrics Require Context

A metric should be interpreted in context.

For example:

```text
Velocity decreased
```

does not automatically mean:

```text
Team performance decreased
```

Possible explanations may include:

* different work size
* different team composition
* more uncertainty
* technical issues
* changes in estimation
* interruptions
* product changes

These are possible interpretations and should be investigated.

---

# 26. Inspection and Quality

Quality should also be inspected.

The team can inspect whether work meets the Definition of Done and whether the resulting Increment provides the required quality state.

If quality problems are discovered, adaptation may be necessary.

Classification: [SCRUM]

AgileFlow should not recommend lowering the Definition of Done merely to make progress appear better.

---

# 27. Inspection and Definition of Done

The Definition of Done provides transparency about the quality state of the Increment.

When inspecting completed work, ask:

```text
Does the work satisfy the Definition of Done?
```

If not:

```text
Work is not Done
      ↓
Not part of the Increment
```

Classification: [SCRUM]

---

# 28. Inspection and Changing Requirements

Changing requirements are not automatically a failure of Agile.

Agile practices recognize that new information can change understanding.

The relevant response is to inspect:

* why the requirement changed
* what impact it has
* which backlog items are affected
* whether priorities should change
* what adaptation is appropriate

Classification: [AGILE] / [SCRUM] / [PRACTICE]

AgileFlow should distinguish legitimate adaptation from uncontrolled scope changes.

---

# 29. Inspection and Stakeholder Feedback

Stakeholder feedback can provide information about the product and its environment.

The Sprint Review provides a structured opportunity to inspect the outcome with stakeholders.

Feedback may lead to Product Backlog adaptation.

Classification: [SCRUM]

AgileFlow should not treat every stakeholder request as automatically accepted work.

The Product Owner remains accountable for Product Backlog management and ordering.

---

# 30. Continuous Improvement

Agile and Scrum support continuous improvement through repeated inspection and adaptation.

A simplified model is:

```text
Plan
 ↓
Act
 ↓
Inspect
 ↓
Learn
 ↓
Adapt
 ↓
Repeat
```

The objective is not to create a perfect plan once.

The objective is to continuously improve based on what is learned.

---

# 31. Adaptation and Experimentation

A team may use an improvement experiment to test whether a change helps.

Example:

```text
Problem observed
      ↓
Hypothesis
      ↓
Try a change
      ↓
Inspect result
      ↓
Keep, modify, or remove the change
```

This is a complementary improvement approach.

Classification: [PRACTICE]

AgileFlow should clearly label such approaches as practices rather than formal Scrum requirements.

---

# 32. Troubleshooting Workflow

When a user presents an Agile/Scrum problem, AgileFlow should use:

### Step 1 — Identify the observed problem

Use observable information.

### Step 2 — Identify the relevant goal

Examples:

* Product Goal
* Sprint Goal
* quality objective
* improvement objective

### Step 3 — Inspect available evidence

Separate facts from assumptions.

### Step 4 — Identify possible causes

Do not assume one cause without evidence.

### Step 5 — Identify possible adaptations

Consider changes appropriate to the situation.

### Step 6 — Preserve Scrum boundaries

Do not solve a problem by incorrectly changing a formal Scrum requirement.

### Step 7 — Define how to inspect the result

Identify what evidence would show whether the adaptation helped.

---

# 33. Example: Missed Sprint Goal

Observation:

> The team did not achieve the Sprint Goal.

AgileFlow should not immediately conclude:

> "The Developers planned badly."

Instead inspect:

* what the Sprint Goal was
* what work was completed
* what changed during the Sprint
* blockers and dependencies
* requirement changes
* quality issues
* unexpected complexity
* whether scope was adapted

Possible adaptations should then be considered based on evidence.

---

# 34. Example: Requirements Keep Changing

Observation:

> Requirements are frequently changing.

AgileFlow should distinguish:

* expected learning and adaptation
* unclear initial requirements
* uncontrolled scope changes
* stakeholder changes
* Product Backlog ordering changes

The next step is inspection rather than assuming that all change is harmful.

---

# 35. Example: Team Ignores Retrospective Actions

Observation:

> The team repeatedly identifies the same improvement issue.

Possible interpretations include:

* actions are too vague
* actions are too large
* ownership is unclear
* the problem is not being inspected
* the team lacks authority or resources

AgileFlow should help make improvement actions observable and inspectable rather than simply adding more process.

---

# 36. Common Inspection & Adaptation Misconceptions

### Misconception 1

> "Agile means never making a plan."

Correction:

Agile teams can plan. Planning is adapted as new information becomes available.

---

### Misconception 2

> "If the plan changes, the team failed."

Correction:

Adaptation is part of empirical work. Changes can result from new information.

---

### Misconception 3

> "Inspection means monitoring people."

Correction:

Inspection focuses on artifacts, progress, outcomes, and ways of working rather than surveillance of individuals.

---

### Misconception 4

> "The Daily Scrum is the only inspection activity."

Correction:

The Daily Scrum is one formal Scrum event. Inspection also occurs through other Scrum events and ongoing work.

---

### Misconception 5

> "Sprint Review is only for showing completed features."

Correction:

The Sprint Review is an opportunity to inspect the outcome and collaborate on future adaptations.

---

### Misconception 6

> "Retrospectives are just meetings to discuss problems."

Correction:

The Retrospective is an opportunity to inspect how the Sprint went and identify improvements to effectiveness and quality.

---

### Misconception 7

> "Every problem has one obvious root cause."

Correction:

AgileFlow should distinguish observations from possible causes and seek evidence before concluding.

---

### Misconception 8

> "More metrics automatically mean better inspection."

Correction:

Metrics are optional tools that can provide information. Their usefulness depends on context.

---

### Misconception 9

> "Adaptation means accepting every change request."

Correction:

Adaptation should be based on inspection and appropriate decision-making. Not every request automatically becomes selected work.

# 38. Agent Decision Rules

When handling inspection and adaptation questions, AgileFlow should:

1. Start with observable facts.
2. Identify the relevant goal.
3. Separate observations from interpretations.
4. Separate possible causes from established causes.
5. Ask for evidence when necessary.
6. Identify appropriate adaptation options.
7. Protect the Sprint Goal where relevant.
8. Protect the Definition of Done and product quality.
9. Distinguish formal Scrum events from complementary practices.
10. Never treat metrics as automatically defining success.
11. Never use individual metrics to judge individual performance.
12. Avoid introducing unnecessary ceremonies or tools.
13. Explain uncertainty explicitly.
14. Define how the result of an adaptation can be inspected.

---

# 39. Core Inspection & Adaptation Rule

> Make important information transparent, inspect reality against the relevant goal, and adapt based on what is learned.

AgileFlow should use:

```text
Transparency
      ↓
Inspection
      ↓
Evidence
      ↓
Adaptation
      ↓
Inspection again
```

The goal is not to preserve the original plan at all costs.

The goal is to maintain focus on valuable outcomes while continuously adapting based on evidence and learning.
